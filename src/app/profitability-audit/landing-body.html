Profitability Analyses

7 Financial Analyses Exposing Profit Leaks

Every dollar of revenue traced from gross sale to net profit.

1

True Product-Level Profit Margin

Objective: Identify Real Profit Per Product

Data Analyzed: COGS, shipping costs, returns, platform fees, discounts, and allocated advertising costs per product.

We calculate the true profit margin of each product after accounting for the major costs behind every sale. This reveals which products are genuinely profitable and which high-revenue products may be generating surprisingly little profit.

Example Insight: “Product A generates strong revenue but delivers only a 2% true profit margin, while Product B produces a much healthier 35% margin. Revenue alone is hiding where your real profit is coming from.”
Strategic Recommendation: Review pricing, costs, and marketing spend on high-revenue, low-margin products before continuing to scale them.

Revenue Trend vs. Profit Trend

Objective: Identify the Gap Between Growth and Profit

Data Analyzed: Monthly revenue growth compared with monthly net profit growth.

We compare revenue and profit trends to reveal whether your business is actually becoming more profitable as it grows. If revenue is rising much faster than profit, we identify what is absorbing the additional revenue and where the gap can be closed.

Example Insight: “Revenue grew 40%, but net profit increased by only 5%. Your business is growing, but most of the additional revenue is being absorbed by rising costs.”
Strategic Recommendation: Identify the costs and margin pressures behind the gap and prioritize actions that turn revenue growth into stronger bottom-line profit.

Cash Flow Pattern Analysis

Objective: Identify Cash Flow Pressure Points

Data Analyzed: Historical cash inflows and outflows, revenue timing, inventory purchases, and major operating expenses.

We identify when cash flow becomes tight and which expenses or timing patterns create pressure on available cash. This helps you plan inventory purchases, marketing spend, and other major expenses around actual cash availability.

Example Insight: “December generated a $24K positive cash flow, while March dropped to just $4K as inventory purchases and operating expenses increased. This pattern shows when cash availability is most likely to come under pressure.”
Strategic Recommendation: Adjust inventory purchasing and major marketing spend around cash flow patterns to maintain healthy liquidity during lower-cash periods.

Seasonal Profitability Analysis

Objective: Identify When Your Business Makes the Most Profit

Data Analyzed: Monthly revenue, costs, and profit trends across seasonal periods.

We compare revenue peaks with profit peaks to identify when your business generates the most sales versus when it actually generates the most profit. This reveals seasonal periods where high sales may not translate into equally strong profitability.

Example Insight: “November generated the highest revenue at $120K, but January produced the highest profit at $31K because lower discounting and stronger margins offset the lower sales volume.”
Strategic Recommendation: Plan promotions, inventory, and marketing spend around your most profitable periods rather than optimizing solely for peak revenue months.

Break-Even Analysis

Objective: Know Your Minimum Revenue Requirement

Data Analyzed: Fixed costs, variable costs, pricing, and monthly revenue.

We calculate the minimum revenue your business needs to cover its operating costs and show how far your actual performance is above or below that point. This gives you a clear baseline for understanding your profitability and financial safety margin.

Example Insight: “Your monthly break-even point is $35K, while actual revenue is $40K. You are operating $5K above break-even, giving you a 14% revenue cushion before costs begin to exceed revenue.”
Strategic Recommendation: Protect your break-even cushion by controlling fixed overhead and variable costs, especially during lower-revenue periods.
