What We Analyze

6 Lenses on Why Customers Leave

We map exactly what brings them back using behavioral data.

1

Customer Acquisition Quality Analysis

Objective: Identify High-Retention Acquisition Channels

Data Analyzed: Acquisition channel vs. repeat purchase behavior.

Not all new customers are equal. We analyze which acquisition channels bring customers who return and purchase again versus those who buy once and leave. This reveals which channels are generating stronger retention and long-term customer value.

Example Insight: “42% of customers acquired through Google made repeat purchases, compared with only 12% of Facebook-acquired customers. Google is bringing customers with significantly stronger retention potential.”
Strategic Recommendation: Prioritize acquisition channels that consistently bring customers with stronger repeat-purchase behavior and long-term value.

Retention Rate Calculation

Objective: Understand How Many Customers Come Back

Data Analyzed: 30-day, 60-day, and 90-day customer retention.

We measure how many customers come back and purchase again after their first purchase. We compare your retention rate with a relevant benchmark to show where you stand and how much additional revenue you could unlock by getting more customers to return.

Example Insight: “Only 15% of your customers return within 30 days, compared with a 25% benchmark. Increasing your 30-day retention could unlock significant additional revenue from your existing customers.”

Full RFM Customer Segmentation

Objective: Understand Customer Behavior & Retention Opportunities

Data Analyzed: Recency, Frequency, and Monetary value for all customers.

We segment your entire customer base into 8 behavioral groups: Champions, Loyal, Potential Loyalists, At-Risk, Hibernating, Lost, New, and Promising. Each segment receives a specific retention action based on their purchasing behavior.

Example Insight: “127 Champions generate 43% of your revenue, while 89 At-Risk customers are showing signs of becoming inactive. Here’s exactly who to retain, re-engage, and win back.”
Strategic Recommendation: Reward Champions and Loyal customers, nurture Potential Loyalists, and launch targeted win-back campaigns for At-Risk and Lost customers.

Churn Pattern Analysis

Objective: Identify Customers Likely to Leave

Data Analyzed: Purchase history, purchase frequency, recency, and engagement behavior of customers who stop buying.

We identify the common behaviors and patterns that appear before customers stop purchasing. This helps us find customers who are showing strong signs of leaving, so you can reach them before they become inactive.

Example Insight: “Customers who haven't purchased in 90+ days and previously bought only once are significantly more likely to leave. These customers should be prioritized for re-engagement.”

Customer Behavior Flow Analysis

Objective: Understand What Customers Do Between Purchases

Data Analyzed: Customer browsing, product views, and cart activity between purchases.

We track what customers do after their purchase and before they buy again, including what they browse, what they add to cart but don't purchase, and where they stop engaging. This helps us understand what may be preventing customers from coming back.

Example Insight: “400 customers are browsing products without adding anything to their cart, while 120 customers are adding products but leaving without purchasing. These groups represent clear opportunities for targeted re-engagement.”
Strategic Recommendation: Use personalized product recommendations, reminder campaigns, and targeted offers to bring inactive shoppers back and encourage another purchase.
