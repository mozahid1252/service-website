Specialized Add-On · Package 03: Product Profitability & Portfolio Audit

Know Which Products Deserve Investment, Improvement, or Reduction

A data-backed review of your product catalog to identify profitability signals, portfolio priorities, pricing opportunities, inventory risks, return patterns, and bundle potential.

A product that generates strong revenue is not always a profitable product. Discounts, product costs, shipping, returns, refunds, inventory holding costs, and operational expenses can significantly change the real value of a product.

The Product Profitability & Portfolio Audit reviews your catalog to help you understand which products are creating value, which products may be creating risk, and where your next product or inventory decision should focus.

Audit My Product Portfolio See the Warning Signs

This is a product and catalog diagnostic. Pricing changes, purchasing decisions, inventory liquidation, development, and ongoing product management are scoped separately.

Warning Signs

Is Your Product Portfolio Showing These Signs?

If several of these feel familiar, your product decisions may be relying on incomplete information.

  • You have many products but do not know which deserve attention
  • Your best-selling products may not be your most profitable
  • Revenue is concentrated in a small number of SKUs
  • Some products sell slowly or tie up inventory
  • Discounts are necessary to move certain products
  • Product returns or refunds are reducing margin
  • You are unsure which products to reorder or reduce
  • You want to create bundles but do not know which products belong together
  • Product pricing has not been reviewed using cost and demand data
  • Your catalog is growing faster than your ability to manage it
  • Inventory decisions are being made mainly from revenue or intuition
  • You do not have a clear product priority or classification system
The Outcome

What This Audit Helps You Understand

A clearer view of product value, portfolio priorities, and catalog risks.

Which products and categories contribute the most value

Which products show strong or weak margin signals

Which products generate revenue but may reduce profitability

Which products appear to require pricing or offer review

Which products are moving quickly or slowly

Where inventory may be tying up cash

Which products may require stock or purchasing attention

Analysis

What We Analyze

Eight connected analyses covering portfolio prioritization, profitability signals, category performance, pricing, inventory efficiency, slow-moving stock, bundle opportunities, and return patterns.

01

Product Classification & Portfolio Prioritization

Objective: Understand Which Products Matter Most to the Business

We classify products according to their contribution to available revenue, sales volume, profitability signals, and strategic importance.

We may review:

  • Product revenue
  • Units sold
  • Order frequency
  • Sales trend
  • Margin signals
  • Return and refund signals
  • Product concentration
  • Category contribution
  • Customer demand signals
  • Available inventory data

Products may be grouped into practical priority tiers, such as:

  • A-Tier: High-priority products that drive meaningful business value
  • B-Tier: Products requiring optimization or monitoring
  • C-Tier: Products with weaker contribution or higher risk

You will learn:

  • Which products deserve protection and investment
  • Which products need optimization
  • Which products require further investigation
  • Which products may need a pricing, bundle, inventory, or portfolio decision
Note: ABC classification is a prioritization framework. It does not automatically mean that every C-Tier product should be discontinued.
02

Product-Level Profitability Signals

Objective: Separate Revenue Performance From Profit Contribution

We review available product-cost and order data to understand how much value products may create after relevant costs.

We may review:

  • Product cost or COGS
  • Gross-margin signals
  • Discount impact
  • Refund and return impact
  • Shipping-cost signals
  • Payment-cost signals
  • Fulfilment-cost signals where available
  • Product-level contribution-margin signals
  • Revenue-to-margin differences

You will learn:

  • Which products show stronger profitability signals
  • Which products may be high-revenue but low-margin
  • Which costs are reducing product contribution
  • Which products require more accurate cost data
  • Where pricing offer bundle or product-mix review may be useful
Note: True profit margin can only be calculated when the relevant cost data is reliable and complete. Where important costs are missing, findings will be labelled as estimates or profitability signals.
03

Category Performance Analysis

Objective: Identify Growing Declining and High-Priority Product Categories

We compare categories across sales, revenue, growth trend, profitability signals, returns, and available inventory information.

We may review:

  • Category revenue
  • Units sold
  • Category growth or decline
  • Product concentration within each category
  • Category margin signals
  • Discount dependence
  • Return and refund patterns
  • Inventory exposure
  • Customer demand signals

You will learn:

  • Which categories show stronger momentum
  • Whether category growth is translating into healthy margin
  • Which categories may need pricing or product-mix review
  • Where product investment may be more appropriate
04

Pricing & Offer Analysis

Objective: Identify Products That May Need Pricing or Offer Review

We review current pricing alongside cost, sales, conversion, discount, and historical performance data where available.

We may review:

  • Current selling price
  • Product cost
  • Gross-margin signals
  • Sales volume
  • Conversion signals
  • Discount depth and frequency
  • Price changes over time
  • Product demand signals
  • Competitor or market pricing if separately provided

You will learn:

  • Which products may have limited margin at the current price
  • Which products may have room for a pricing test
  • Where discounting may be reducing profitability
  • Which products may benefit from a bundle or offer change

Recommendations will be framed as:

  • Pricing review
  • Controlled price test
  • Offer test
  • Bundle test
  • Maintain current price
  • Further investigation
Note: A recommended price is not a guaranteed optimal price. Actual price changes should be tested against demand, conversion, margin, and customer response.
05

Inventory Efficiency Analysis

Objective: Understand Which Products May Be Moving Quickly or Slowly

Where inventory and purchasing data are available, we compare sales velocity with current stock and available replenishment information.

We may review:

  • Units sold
  • Sales velocity
  • Current inventory
  • Days of inventory on hand
  • Stockout signals
  • Overstock signals
  • Reorder status
  • Product availability
  • Purchase or replenishment data where available

You will learn:

  • Which products may require replenishment attention
  • Which products may be overstocked
  • Which products are tying up cash
  • Which inventory decisions deserve further review
Note: This analysis requires reliable inventory data. Supplier lead times, purchase-order management, warehouse operations, and demand planning are not included in the base audit.
06

Slow-Moving and Dead-Stock Signals

Objective: Identify Products That May Be Tying Up Cash

We review products with limited sales movement over a defined period and compare their available inventory value, age, and demand signals.

We may review:

  • Units sold during the selected period
  • Days since last sale
  • Current inventory value
  • Inventory age
  • Holding-cost signals where available
  • Discount history
  • Return or quality concerns
  • Product demand trend
  • Potential clearance or bundling options

You will learn:

  • Which products show slow-moving or dead-stock risk
  • How much inventory value may be tied up
  • Which products may be suitable for clearance bundling or further review
  • Which products should not receive additional purchasing priority
Note: Estimated recovery value is directional. Actual recovery depends on customer demand, pricing, discounting, inventory condition, and execution.
07

Cross-Sell & Bundle Opportunity Analysis

Objective: Identify Products Frequently Purchased Together

We review order-level product combinations to identify potential bundles and cross-sell opportunities.

We may review:

  • Products purchased in the same order
  • Purchase frequency of product pairs
  • Category relationships
  • Product margin signals
  • Bundle price feasibility
  • AOV signals
  • Attach-rate signals
  • Customer purchase patterns

You will learn:

  • Which products show natural purchase relationships
  • Which combinations may be suitable for bundles
  • Which cross-sell opportunities may support AOV
  • Which combinations should be tested first

Possible recommendation types:

  • Bundle test
  • Cart cross-sell
  • Product-page cross-sell
  • Post-purchase recommendation
  • Complementary offer
  • No bundle recommendation due to weak evidence
Note: Potential AOV impact is not guaranteed. Actual uplift must be measured after the bundle or cross-sell offer is implemented.
08

Product Return & Refund Analysis

Objective: Identify Products Losing Value Through Returns and Refunds

We review product-level return and refund patterns to understand where strong sales may be accompanied by avoidable losses.

We may review:

  • Product return rate
  • Refund amount
  • Return reasons
  • Product-level return concentration
  • Category-level return patterns
  • Discounted versus non-discounted returns
  • Customer or market differences where available
  • Available margin impact

You will learn:

  • Which products show unusually high return or refund signals
  • How returns may affect product profitability
  • Which products require investigation
  • Whether product information sizing quality packaging or customer expectations may need review
Note: This audit identifies patterns. It does not include product quality testing, supplier investigation, warehouse inspection, or customer-support process redesign.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

Who is this audit for?
It is best suited for e-commerce businesses with enough product, sales, cost, and inventory data to make product-level decisions.
Do I need 50 or more SKUs?
No. The audit may be useful with fewer SKUs if product profitability, pricing, inventory, or returns are important business questions. The required scope depends on catalog complexity and data availability.
Can you calculate true profit for every product?
We can calculate or estimate product profitability where reliable COGS and relevant cost data are available. If shipping, returns, fulfilment, payment, or marketing costs are missing, the result will be labelled as a profitability signal or estimate.
Will you tell me exactly which products to discontinue?
We may identify products for reduction, clearance, or further investigation when the evidence supports that direction. Final discontinuation decisions also depend on brand strategy, customer demand, supplier constraints, and future product plans.
Can you recommend new prices?
We can identify products suitable for price review or controlled price testing. A recommended price is not a guarantee of higher profit or stable demand.
Will you find dead stock?
We can identify slow-moving or dead-stock signals when reliable inventory and sales data are available. A full inventory audit or warehouse review is not included.
Can you guarantee bundle or AOV improvement?
No. We identify product combinations with supporting purchase patterns and recommend how they may be tested. Actual AOV impact must be measured after implementation.
Will you manage inventory or purchasing?
No. The audit provides priorities and recommendations. Daily inventory management, purchasing, supplier coordination, and warehouse operations are separate services.
Are returns and refunds included?
Yes, where product-level return, refund, and reason data is available. The analysis identifies patterns and potential impact; it does not include product testing or operational investigation.
Is the automated product dashboard included?
No. It is an optional data automation project and is scoped separately.
Can I add an automated dashboard?
Yes. An automated dashboard and data pipeline can be scoped separately through the Automate Your Data Infrastructure & Dashboards service.
How long does the audit take?
The audit is typically delivered within 7–10 business days after the required data and access are received.
