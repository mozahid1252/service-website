Deep Health Check Audit

Go Beyond Surface-Level Signals and Understand What Is Really Driving Your Business

A comprehensive diagnostic for established e-commerce brands with larger datasets, multiple channels, and complex growth questions.

Your business may be generating revenue while hidden issues affect profitability, marketing efficiency, customer value, conversion, and future growth. The Deep E-Commerce Health Check Audit examines your business across a longer historical period and a deeper level of product, channel, customer, funnel, profitability, and data analysis. You will receive a clear view of what is working, what is creating risk, where the largest business impact exists, and what should be addressed first.

Warning Signs

Is Your Business Complex Enough for a Deep Health Check?

If several of these apply, your business likely needs a deeper diagnostic rather than a quick snapshot.

  • Your store has a large volume of orders, customers, or products
  • You have multiple marketing channels
  • Revenue and profit do not appear to move together
  • Different platforms report significantly different results
  • You need more than a recent 90-day snapshot
  • You are unsure which products, channels, or customer groups create real value
  • Conversion performance varies significantly by device, channel, product, or market
  • Your business has enough historical data for deeper customer or cohort analysis
  • You are preparing for a major budget, product, or growth decision
  • A Standard Health Check has identified issues that require deeper investigation
  • Your team needs a more detailed decision roadmap before investing in implementation

What We Analyze

11 Deep Business Health Areas

Each area is reviewed in depth to identify root-cause patterns, business impact, and recommended priorities.

1

Historical Revenue & Sales Health

Understand How the Business Has Changed Over Time

We review your historical sales performance across a longer period to understand the direction, concentration, and durability of your business.

We examine:

  • Monthly revenue trend
  • Order-volume trend
  • Average order value
  • Units per order (where available)
  • New versus returning customer revenue
  • Revenue concentration
  • Product and category contribution
  • Seasonal patterns
  • Growth and decline periods
  • Unusual spikes or drops
  • Revenue dependence on a small number of products or channels
You will learn: Whether growth is consistent or concentrated; Which periods require further investigation; Whether revenue changes are driven by volume, pricing, product mix, or customer mix; Whether recent performance represents a meaningful trend or a temporary fluctuation.
Example insight: "Revenue increased over the past six months, but most of the growth came from a limited number of products and one acquisition channel, creating concentration risk."
2

Profitability & Margin Health

Identify Where Revenue Does—and Does Not—Translate Into Profit

Revenue does not always equal profit. We use the available cost and order data to identify where margin is created and where it may be lost.

We examine:

  • Product cost or COGS
  • Gross-margin signals
  • Contribution-margin signals
  • Discount impact
  • Refund and return impact
  • Shipping and fulfilment costs (where available)
  • Payment-processing costs (where available)
  • Product-level margin differences
  • Category-level margin differences
  • High-revenue but low-margin products
  • Margin concentration
  • Available channel-level cost impact
You will learn: Which products appear most profitable; Which products may be overvalued based on revenue alone; Where discounts, returns, or operational costs reduce margin; Which profitability questions require better cost data.
Example insight: "A high-volume product contributes significant revenue but produces weaker contribution-margin signals than a lower-volume product after discounts, returns, and available fulfilment costs are considered."
Note: Full accounting, bookkeeping, tax analysis, and guaranteed profit forecasting are not included.
3

Product & Portfolio Health

Understand Which Products Deserve More, Less, or Different Attention

We compare products and categories across revenue, margin signals, returns, discounts, and momentum to identify where attention should be focused.

We examine:

  • Product and category revenue
  • Units sold
  • Sales velocity
  • Product growth and decline
  • Product-level margin signals
  • Product revenue concentration
  • Product return and refund patterns
  • Product discount dependence
  • Product availability signals
  • Bundle and cross-sell opportunities
  • Category-level contribution
  • Product investment priorities

Initial product directions may include:

  • Grow - Maintain - Investigate - Reprice - Bundle - Reduce - Retire (subject to sufficient evidence)
You will learn: Which products are driving growth; Which products appear to create margin risk; Which categories are gaining or losing momentum; Where product-level investigation or implementation should begin.
Note: Full inventory, warehouse, supplier, purchase-order, demand-planning, or dead-stock analysis is not included unless separately scoped.
4

Marketing Channel Efficiency & Profitability

Compare Marketing Channels Using More Than Platform-Reported ROAS

We compare channels using available spend, attributed revenue, acquisition, conversion, and profitability signals instead of relying on platform-reported ROAS alone.

We examine:

  • Meta, Google, Email, Organic, Direct, Referral, Other available channels
  • Channel spend
  • Attributed revenue
  • Reported ROAS
  • CAC signals
  • New-customer acquisition
  • Returning-customer contribution
  • Channel-level conversion
  • Available product-cost and margin impact
  • Attribution limitations
  • Channel concentration and dependency
You will learn: Which channels appear efficient; Which channels generate revenue without clear business profit; Which channels may be over- or under-credited; Whether acquisition performance differs from customer-value performance; Which channel questions require a specialist audit or implementation.
Example insight: "One channel shows strong platform-reported ROAS, but its performance is concentrated among returning visitors, while another channel brings more new customers at a higher initial CAC."
Note: Detailed campaign, creative, audience, placement, and daily media-buying work is not included.
5

Tracking, Attribution & Data Reconciliation

Understand Which Numbers Can Support Important Decisions

We reconcile the differences between store, analytics, and marketing-platform data to understand which numbers are reliable for important decisions.

We examine:

  • Shopify or e-commerce-platform orders
  • GA4 purchases and revenue
  • Meta-reported conversions and revenue (where applicable)
  • Google Ads conversions and revenue (where applicable)
  • Event availability
  • Missing or duplicate conversion signals
  • Transaction-value consistency
  • UTM and source/medium quality
  • Attribution-window differences
  • New versus returning customer classification
  • Date and timezone differences
  • Reporting-definition differences
  • Data-source completeness
  • Important data limitations
You will learn: Which source should be used as the commercial baseline; Why platform numbers may not match; Which metrics are suitable for which decisions; Whether a tracking issue may materially affect budget or performance decisions; Whether a dedicated Tracking & Attribution Audit is required.
Note: This audit documents and interprets measurement issues. Full GTM, GA4, Pixel, CAPI, server-side implementation, and technical repair are separate services.
6

Funnel, CRO & Conversion Health

Find Where Conversion Performance Changes Across the Customer Journey

We review the customer journey across devices, sources, products, and visitor segments to identify where conversion performance changes most.

We examine:

  • Sessions or landing-page visits
  • Product views
  • Add-to-cart activity
  • Checkout initiation
  • Purchase completion
  • Landing-page progression
  • Product-page progression
  • Cart-to-checkout progression
  • Checkout-to-purchase completion
  • Mobile versus desktop
  • Traffic-source funnel performance
  • Campaign-level funnel signals (where available)
  • Product-level conversion
  • New versus returning visitor behaviour
  • Available geographic or market differences
You will learn: Which funnel stage creates the largest potential loss; Whether conversion weakness is concentrated in a device, channel, product, or visitor segment; Whether the issue appears to be traffic quality, page experience, offer, cart, or checkout; Whether a full E-Commerce CRO Audit should follow.
Note: This is a deeper diagnostic, not a full page-by-page CRO audit, UX redesign, copywriting engagement, or development project.
7

Customer, Retention & LTV Health

Understand Which Customers Create Long-Term Value

We review customer behaviour, repeat purchase, and customer-value signals to identify which customers drive long-term value.

We examine:

  • New versus returning customers
  • Repeat-purchase rate
  • Purchase frequency
  • Time between purchases
  • Customer revenue concentration
  • Customer-value distribution
  • Customer acquisition source
  • Basic LTV signals
  • Customer inactivity signals
  • Available customer segments
  • Retention trend over time
  • Acquisition cohort behaviour (where data supports it)
You will learn: Whether the business depends heavily on new customer acquisition; Which customers or acquisition sources appear to create greater long-term value; Whether repeat purchase is improving or weakening; Where retention or customer-value improvement may be available; Whether a dedicated Retention & LTV engagement is appropriate.
Example insight: "Customers acquired during one period show stronger repeat purchase and higher available value signals than customers acquired through another channel, suggesting a quality—not only volume—difference in acquisition."
Note: Full RFM activation, churn prediction, email/SMS automation, and customer-platform implementation are not included.
8

Cohort & Retention Trend Analysis

Separate Temporary Customer Behaviour From Persistent Retention Problems

We use cohort analysis to distinguish temporary customer behaviour from persistent retention problems over time.

We examine:

  • Acquisition cohorts
  • Repeat-purchase behaviour over time
  • Revenue contribution by cohort
  • Purchase frequency
  • Retention trend
  • Available LTV progression
  • Cohort differences by channel or product
  • Changes in customer quality over time
You will learn: Which customer groups retain better; Whether retention is improving or deteriorating; Whether a growth period brought valuable or low-value customers; Which cohort or acquisition question deserves deeper investigation.
Note: Cohort analysis requires sufficient historical order data. If the available history is limited, the finding will be marked as limited confidence.
9

Discount, Refund & Return Impact

Identify How Promotions and Post-Purchase Issues Affect Business Health

We review how promotions, refunds, and returns affect revenue, margin, and customer value across the business.

We examine:

  • Discount usage
  • Discount depth
  • Discounted versus non-discounted orders
  • Revenue lift signals
  • Margin impact
  • Refund rate
  • Return rate
  • Product-level return patterns
  • Category-level return patterns
  • Available return reasons
  • Lost revenue and margin signals
  • Customer or channel differences (where available)
You will learn: Whether discounts appear to support profitable growth; Where promotion activity may reduce margin; Which products show unusual return or refund patterns; Whether a product, offer, or customer-experience issue requires further action.
Note: Supplier, warehouse, carrier, and physical fulfilment investigations are not included.
10

Product, Customer & Channel Cross-Analysis

Connect the Business Signals Instead of Reviewing Them in Isolation

We connect product, customer, and channel signals so that one metric is not reviewed in isolation from the others.

We examine:

  • Which channels sell which products
  • Which products attract repeat customers
  • Which products are purchased by high-value customers
  • Which products have strong revenue but weak margin signals
  • Which channels generate high-value versus low-value customers
  • Which customer groups respond to specific offers
  • Which products may support bundles or cross-sell opportunities
You will learn: Where product, customer, and marketing performance reinforce each other; Where one metric looks strong but another reveals a risk; Which growth opportunities may be more commercially valuable; Which business question should be investigated next.

FAQ

Frequently Asked Questions

What is the difference between the Standard and Deep Health Check?
The Standard Health Check is a rapid, high-level diagnostic designed to identify the most important business risks and priorities. The Deep Health Check uses more historical data and provides deeper product, profitability, channel, customer, cohort, funnel, and reconciliation analysis for larger or more complex businesses.
Do I need to complete the Standard Health Check first?
No. You may start directly with the Deep Health Check if your business already has sufficient data and you know that a deeper analysis is required.
Is this a full profitability audit?
It provides deeper profitability analysis using the available product, order, discount, refund, return, shipping, payment, and cost data. It is not bookkeeping, tax, statutory accounting, or a replacement for audited financial statements.
Is this a full CRO audit?
No. The Deep Health Check investigates funnel and conversion patterns at a business-diagnostic level. A detailed page-by-page E-Commerce CRO Audit can be scoped separately.
Is this a full Meta or Google Ads audit?
No. The Deep Health Check reviews channel-level performance and available profitability signals. Detailed campaign, audience, creative, placement, and media-buying analysis requires a separate specialist scope.
Will you fix the problems found?
No. The Deep Health Check identifies root-cause patterns, priorities, and recommended next steps. Implementation is available separately through E-Commerce Performance Improvement.
Can you calculate LTV and cohort performance?
Yes, where sufficient customer-level order history and reliable acquisition-source data are available. If the data is incomplete, findings will be marked with the appropriate confidence level.
Can you forecast future revenue or demand?
The Deep Health Check may identify trends and scenario-based planning signals, but it does not include a guaranteed forecast or full demand-planning system.
Will Shopify, GA4, and advertising-platform numbers match exactly?
Not necessarily. Different platforms use different attribution rules, time windows, consent signals, and reporting definitions. We reconcile the differences and explain which source is appropriate for each decision.
What if my data is incomplete?
We document the limitation, reduce the confidence of the affected findings, and identify the most important data improvement required.
Does this include a dashboard?
No live dashboard is included in the base Deep Health Check. A dashboard or monitoring system can be scoped separately.
How long does the audit take?
The Deep Health Check is typically delivered within 15-20 business days after the required data and access are received.
